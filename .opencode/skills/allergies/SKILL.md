# Allergies and dietary needs

## Reading the guest list

Allergy notes are free text written by the guests. Interpret each one into the allergens it covers before planning:

- "nuts" covers **peanuts** and **tree nuts**; "shellfish" covers **crustaceans** and **molluscs**; "coeliac" means **gluten**, including cross-contamination.
- Severity words (severe, anaphylaxis, EpiPen) make an allergy **severe**.
- A dislike ("doesn't like spicy food", "feel better without") is a **preference**: offer a fitting option where it's easy, and keep the menu built around the real allergies.
- Check the `notes` field too; guests sometimes write dietary needs there.

Plan for guests whose RSVP is `attending`. For each `pending` guest with an allergy, add a menu note that says how the kitchen will adapt if they confirm ("If Elena confirms: …"). Guests who have `declined` are left out entirely.

## Menu rules

1. **Severe means kitchen-wide.** A severe allergen appears in no dish at all, including garnishes, desserts and drinks.
2. **Every guest, every course.** Each attending guest has at least one dish they can safely eat in every course (starter, main, dessert, drinks), respecting both allergies and diet (vegan, vegetarian, pescatarian).
3. **Alternatives say who they are for.** When a dish exists for specific guests, its `notes` name them ("Vegan and nut-free: for Lena").
4. **Label with the EU 14.** List allergens on every item using exactly these names: celery, cereals containing gluten, crustaceans, eggs, fish, lupin, milk, molluscs, mustard, tree nuts, peanuts, sesame, soya, sulphites. An item with none has an empty list.
5. **Dietary tags** use: vegan, vegetarian, pescatarian, gluten-free, lactose-free.

## In invitations

Every invitation asks guests to tell the organiser about allergies and dietary needs when they reply, by the RSVP deadline.
